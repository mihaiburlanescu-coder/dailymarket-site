Good to know about the VIX
  • The VIX shows how large a move the market expects in the SP500 over the next 30 days. It usually rises when stocks fall and drifts lower slowly when the market is calm.
  • VIX calls are bought mainly as protection for stock portfolios: buying calls is a bet that fear will rise. That is why a low put/call ratio (0.2–0.4) is normal for the VIX and does not mean optimism.
  • VIX options are priced off VIX futures, not off the index shown here, and they expire on a Wednesday (30 days before the monthly SP500 options expiry). At expiry they settle at a special VIX value calculated on the morning of that day.
  • The VIX tends to revert towards its long-term average (around 19–20). Spikes above 30 happen in moments of panic and usually do not last long.
  • VIX CFDs and ETFs track the futures, not the index. In calm periods the futures trade above the VIX and VIX products gradually lose value (contango), even if the VIX itself stays flat.

What is the “magnet”?
The magnet is the price at which option buyers would lose the most money at expiry: at that price, the most bets expire worthless. On the other side are the option sellers, usually banks and large firms. To hedge their risk they buy and sell in the market, and this tends to pull the price towards the zone where they lose the least, especially in the last few days before expiry and when there is no major news.
How to read it: if the price is close to the magnet before expiry, it may stay “pinned” around it; if the magnet is higher or lower, it shows a slight pull in that direction. It is a reference level, not a prediction: major news can override it at any time.

Good to know about the VIX
  • The VIX shows how large a move the market expects in the SP500 over the next 30 days. It usually rises when stocks fall and drifts lower slowly when the market is calm.
  • VIX calls are bought mainly as protection for stock portfolios: buying calls is a bet that fear will rise. That is why a low put/call ratio (0.2–0.4) is normal for the VIX and does not mean optimism.
  • VIX options are priced off VIX futures, not off the index shown here, and they expire on a Wednesday (30 days before the monthly SP500 options expiry). At expiry they settle at a special VIX value calculated on the morning of that day.
